01 / THE PROPOSITION

Film is a system of relationships.

A camera is only one component. The filmmaker also negotiates reality, people, resources, technology, institutions, spectators and time.

01

SEE

Train attention before technique. Notice what others pass over.

02

RESEARCH

Question sources, memory, context, power and your own position.

03

MAKE

Turn constraints into form. Learn through actual production.

04

REFLECT

Read your own images. Ask what the film reveals and hides.

05

SCREEN

Meet audiences, spaces, criticism and unexpected meanings.

06

CONTINUE

Distribute, preserve, revisit, teach and let the film develop an afterlife.

02 / FILMMAKING LIFECYCLE

From noticing to continuing.

There is no universal order. Independent filmmaking loops, breaks, returns and changes direction.

01 — NOTICE

Observation

Image, sound, place, gesture, memory, contradiction and curiosity.

02 — ASK

Question

Idea, concept, premise, theme, research question and ethical question.

03 — RESEARCH

Encounter

Fieldwork, archive, interview, observation, sources and permissions.

04 — DISCOVER FORM

Design

Genre, mode, duration, structure, visual grammar and sound strategy.

05 — DEVELOP

Write / Prototype

Logline, synopsis, treatment, screenplay, proof of concept.

06 — ORGANIZE

Produce

Team, budget, financing, rights, schedule and co-production.

07 — PREPARE

Pre-production

Breakdown, casting, locations, camera, sound, art and safety.

08 — MAKE

Production

Direction, performance, camera, light, sound, continuity and data.

09 — SHAPE

Post-production

Editing, montage, sound, music, color, VFX and titles.

10 — FINISH

Master

Picture lock, mix, grade, subtitles, accessibility and QC.

11 — POSITION

Public Life

Poster, trailer, press kit, audience strategy and premiere.

12 — CIRCULATE

Screen / Distribute

Festivals, cinema, community, education, streaming and direct audiences.

13 — PRESERVE

Archive

Masters, source media, metadata, checksums, rights and redundancy.

14 — CONTINUE

Afterlife

Restoration, rerelease, research, teaching, retrospective and memory.

05 / HISTORICAL CURRENT

One medium. Many emancipations.

Cinema repeatedly reinvents itself when filmmakers challenge what counts as an image, who can make one and how it can circulate.

1900s–20s

1930s–60s

REALITY / RUPTURE

POLITICAL / PERSONAL

Third cinema, feminist, queer, postcolonial, community and radical media.

2000s → NOW

DIGITAL / NETWORKED

Desktop, mobile, hybrid, streaming, XR and AI-assisted cinema.

07 / INDEPENDENT SPIRIT

Autonomy without romanticizing hardship.

Independence is not poverty, isolation or refusal of collaboration. It is creative agency, responsible labor and meaningful control.

MAKE ANYHOW.
MAKE CONSCIOUSLY.
MAKE TOGETHER.
NO PERMISSION REQUIRED

Start with the resources, people and landscape you actually have.

NO ROMANCE OF EXPLOITATION

Low budget does not justify unsafe, unpaid or disposable labor.

TECHNOLOGY IS A TOOL

Use cameras, phones, software and AI to extend human intention.

REALITY IS NOT RAW MATERIAL

People and places are participants in an ethical encounter.

ARCHIVE THE PROCESS

Rushes, notes, metadata and failures are part of film history.

BUILD YOUR OWN CIRCULATION

Festivals matter, but community, education, web and direct audiences matter too.

What can you make with the resources already around you?

And what new form becomes possible because those resources are limited?

08 / AI-ERA CINEMA

Augment the filmmaker. Do not erase the filmmaker.

AI enters research, writing, translation, image generation, editing, sound, restoration, metadata and distribution. The question is not simply whether AI can make something. The question is who remains responsible for the work.

01 / ASSIST

AI as extension

Brainstorming, transcription, translation, organization, research, technical assistance and iteration.

02 / GENERATE

Synthetic material

Generated images, voices, performances and environments raise questions of authorship, provenance and disclosure.

03 / QUESTION

Whose data?

Training sources, bias, consent, copyright, cultural knowledge and environmental cost remain production questions.

04 / RETURN

Human judgment

The filmmaker remains responsible for selecting, framing, contextualizing and releasing the final work.

09 / HOW TO READ A FILM

Turn viewing into a practice.

Do not only ask whether a film is “good” or “bad”. Ask what it does.

IMAGE

What do I see?

Frame, lens, composition, movement, light, color and texture.

SOUND

What do I hear?

Voice, ambience, silence, music, noise and perspective.

TIME

How long?

Duration, repetition, ellipsis, simultaneity and rhythm.

SPACE

Where?

Architecture, geography, landscape, borders and proximity.

BODY

Who moves?

Gesture, performance, labor, intimacy, vulnerability and agency.

MEANING

What connects?

Motifs, contrasts, metaphors, ambiguity and form.

POWER

Who controls?

Who looks, speaks, edits, owns, funds and distributes?

AFTERLIFE

What remains?

Audience memory, archive, criticism, circulation and disappearance.

10 / THE CENTRAL QUESTIONS

Who controls the image?

The Cinema Knowledge System ultimately returns to a set of questions about power, authorship, labor, memory and access.

WHO SEES?

Who holds the camera? From whose perspective?

WHO IS SEEN?

Who becomes visible? Who remains outside the frame?

WHO SPEAKS?

Who controls voice, testimony and narrative?

WHO OWNS?

Who owns the image, sound, rights and archive?



WHO PAYS?

Who finances the production? What does that influence?

WHO WORKS?

Who performs visible and invisible labor?

WHO WATCHES?

Who is the imagined audience? Who actually encounters the work?

WHO REMEMBERS?

What survives? What disappears?
